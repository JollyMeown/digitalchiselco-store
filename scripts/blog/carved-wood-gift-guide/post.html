Somebody who owns a CNC router has an unfair advantage in December. Everyone else is choosing between a scarf and a gift card. You can put a piece of carved wood in someone's hands that they will hang on a wall and keep for thirty years, and it cost you a board, an evening, and a file.

The catch is choosing the right design for the right person, and starting early enough that it is dry and finished before the day. This guide handles both. It is sorted by who you are giving to, not by what we happen to sell, and every pick comes with the wood that suits it, the size it wants to be, and an honest idea of how long it takes on the machine.

One thing worth knowing about how the list was built. The most searched word on this site all year, by a distance, is fishing, in six different spellings. After that: wedding gift, cottage, bear, duck, horse, celtic, tray. That is not us guessing what people want to give. That is what people came here looking for. The sections follow it.

How to read each pick

Every design below is a bas-relief STL file: a real 3D model with genuine depth, not a V-carve or an engraving. You buy the file once, it downloads instantly, and it carves on any CNC router that runs 3D toolpaths. Most also print on a 3D printer and several engrave on a laser as depth maps. Commercial use is included, so if the gift is such a hit that three friends want one, you can sell them.

For each pick you will see:

  • Wood. What the design looks best in, and what to avoid. If you are new to this, the species table in our finishing guide explains why.
  • Size. The size the design wants to be to hold its detail. Go smaller and fine features disappear under the bit. How to scale an STL without losing detail covers the maths.
  • Machine time. A ballpark for a 1/8 inch tapered ball nose finishing pass at a sensible stepover, on a hobby machine. Your feeds, your machine and your patience will move it. Add a roughing pass and it drops sharply.

Prices shown are for the file itself and are correct at the time of publishing.

A wall panel is admired. A tray is used. If the person you are buying for hosts, a carved serving tray with compartments for cheese, nuts and dried fruit will be on their table every holiday from now on, and it will have your name attached to it every time someone asks where it came from.

One rule for trays: finish them for food. That means food-grade mineral oil or a mineral oil and beeswax blend, not the glaze and varnish you would use on a panel. The food-safe section of the finishing guide has the full method. Keep decorative glaze to the outer border and out of the wells.

The Christmas calendar: when to start

The reason carved gifts arrive late is never the carving. It is the finishing. Oil needs days to cure, glaze needs a day before anything goes over it, and wax wants a day to harden before the piece goes in a box. Work backwards from the day it needs to be wrapped:

Days before givingWhat happens
14 to 10Buy the file, pick the board, cut a test tile on the same board with the same bit. Test your finish schedule on the tile.
10 to 7Carve. Roughing pass, finishing pass, overnight if it is a big one.
7 to 6Fuzz off, sand the high points, raise the grain, seal.
6 to 5Glaze and wipe back. Leave it a full day.
5 to 2Oil or wiping varnish, thin coats a day apart. Clear the puddles each time.
2 to 1Wax, buff, fit the hanger. Let it sit upright overnight.
0Box it.

So the last comfortable day to start a single Christmas gift is around December 10. If you are making several, the machine time stacks and the finishing runs in parallel, so start by November 20 and you will finish relaxed. Start on December 18 and you will be giving someone a tacky panel and an apology.

Presenting it so it lands

You have spent nine hours on this. Do not hand it over in a supermarket bag.

  • A plain kraft box with tissue. Nothing beats it. The wood is the colour; everything around it should be quiet.
  • A card that explains it. Wood species, where the board came from if it has a story, the design's name, and that you carved it. People treasure the object more when they understand it.
  • Fit the hanger. A sawtooth hanger or a keyhole slot on the back, already fitted, means it is on their wall that evening rather than leaning against a skirting board until March.
  • Oil the back too. An unfinished back takes up moisture unevenly and the panel cups. A wipe of the same oil is all it needs.
  • Sign it. Your initials and the year, burned or carved small on the back. In thirty years somebody will look for them.

Questions people ask

Can I add a name or a date?

Yes, and you should. V-carve a single line of text on the flat border as a separate toolpath after the relief is cut. Keep it plain and no smaller than 10 mm tall.

Which wood if I can only buy one board?

Cherry. It carves cleanly, it holds detail, it takes oil beautifully, and it darkens with age so the gift gets better every year.

How big should a gift panel be?

Between 12 and 20 inches for most designs. Under 12 and fine detail starts to vanish under the bit; over 24 and it becomes furniture. Each pick above gives its own range.

How long does it really take?

Carving: three to twelve hours on the machine depending on the design and your stepover. Finishing: five to seven days, mostly waiting. The waiting is the part people forget.

Is a carved tray safe for food?

Finished with food-grade mineral oil or an oil and beeswax blend, yes, for dry foods: cheese, crackers, nuts, fruit. Keep raw meat and knife work on a plain flat board.

Can I sell the ones I make?

Yes. Every file includes commercial use for the physical pieces you carve. The one thing you cannot do is sell or share the file itself.

What if the design I want is not here?

There are over 1,500 in the full catalogue, and if it still is not there, ask for it on the design board. Subscriber requests regularly become real files.

A carved gift says something a bought one cannot: that you thought about this person for nine hours while a machine hummed, and then for another week while the oil cured. Choose the design for the person, give it the finish it deserves, and start before December 10.
